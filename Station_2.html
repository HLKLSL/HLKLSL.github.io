Lagos: Nigeria’s Economic Engine

Lagos is Nigeria’s economic centre, home to major banks, the country’s largest seaport, and many national and international companies. The city attracts millions of people from across West Africa who come in search of economic opportunities.

Much of the city’s economy depends on the informal sector, where street vendors, market traders, artisans, and transport workers provide essential goods and services. This sector is highly dynamic and flexible, allowing people to create income even without formal education or access to formal employment.

Lagos is also a hub of creativity and innovation. Its music, fashion, Nollywood film industry, and growing digital start-up scene have made the city a leading cultural and economic force in Africa.

The Informal Sector in Lagos

The informal sector is the backbone of Lagos’ daily life. It includes millions of workers who operate outside formal employment structures. These activities range from small-scale trading and food vending to transportation services such as motorcycle taxis (okadas) and informal bus systems (danfos).

Despite lacking formal regulation, the informal sector is highly organized and efficient. It provides employment for a large share of Lagos residents and plays a crucial role in keeping the city functioning. Many families rely entirely on informal work for their livelihoods.

Creative Industries & Nollywood

Lagos is the heart of Nigeria’s creative industries. Nollywood, based largely in Lagos, is one of the world’s largest film industries, producing over 2,000 films each year. These films are distributed across Africa and the global African diaspora, making Nollywood a powerful cultural export.

Beyond film, Lagos is a centre for Afrobeats music, fashion design, digital art, and tech innovation. Creative industries contribute significantly to the city’s economy and shape its global identity.

Key Facts About Lagos’ Economy

  • Lagos is home to more than 20 million people, making it one of the largest cities in Africa.
  • The city handles around 70–80% of Nigeria’s maritime trade through its ports.
  • Nigeria’s film industry, Nollywood, produces over 2,000 films each year.
  • The informal sector provides employment for millions of Lagos residents.
  • Lagos generates over 30% of Nigeria’s GDP, despite occupying less than 0.5% of the country’s land area.
