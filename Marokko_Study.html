What is Makoko?

Makoko is a lagoon-side community in Lagos, often described as a “floating neighbourhood”. Many homes are built on wooden stilts above the water, and residents move around using small wooden canoes. The community is estimated to have over 100,000 residents, most of whom rely on fishing, small-scale trade and boat transport for their daily lives.

Why is Makoko Important?

Makoko is important because it shows how people adapt creatively to extreme urban challenges. Despite lacking formal infrastructure such as electricity, sanitation and waste collection, the community has developed its own systems: informal schools, local markets, boat transport networks and strong social support structures. Makoko is also a key example in debates about land rights, urban planning and the future of Lagos as a megacity.

What Does Makoko Reveal About Nigeria?

Makoko reveals the contrasts and complexities of modern Nigeria. It shows how rapid urban growth creates both opportunities and deep inequalities. The community highlights issues such as poverty, informal housing, environmental vulnerability and the struggle for recognition by the government. At the same time, Makoko demonstrates the resilience, innovation and strong community identity that shape many Nigerian cities. It challenges stereotypes by showing that Nigerian urban life is diverse, dynamic and constantly evolving.
