Moving Millions: Transport and Infrastructure in Lagos

Lagos is a city in constant motion. Every day, over 20 million people rely on a mix of roads, waterways, and informal transport to get around — creating one of Africa’s busiest mobility systems. That is as big as New York City.

Traffic as a Way of Life

The city’s legendary “go-slow” can turn short trips into hours-long journeys. On the 11.8 km Third Mainland Bridge, commuters may spend up to 4 hours during peak times. Yet Lagosians turn traffic into opportunity: vendors sell goods between cars, and the road becomes a moving marketplace.

Danfo Buses: The Yellow Lifeline

The iconic yellow danfo minibuses handle over 60% of daily public transport. They are loud, fast, and sometimes chaotic — but remain the most flexible and affordable way for millions to move.

Water Transport: Faster Than the Roads

Built on a lagoon, Lagos depends on its waterways. Modern LAGFERRY boats can reduce a 90-minute road trip to just 20 minutes, offering a reliable alternative to gridlocked highways.

A Glimpse of the Future

The Bus Rapid Transit (BRT) system now carries 200,000+ passengers daily, cutting travel times by up to 40%. For Comparison, in NYC around 1.3m People use the Bus every single day. The new fully electric Lagos Blue Line Rail, launched in 2023, is designed to move 250,000 passengers per day once complete — a major step toward modern urban mobility.

A Growing City, A Growing Challenge

With 600,000 new residents every year, Lagos grows faster than its infrastructure can keep up. Flooding, road wear, and power outages strain the system — yet Lagosians continue to innovate with ride-hailing, motorcycle taxis (okadas), and community-organized transport.

Transport in Lagos is more than movement; it is a story of resilience, creativity, and survival in one of the world’s most dynamic megacities.
