Reflection

This exhibition challenges several common stereotypes about Nigeria by showing Lagos as a city of contrasts rather than a place defined by a single narrative. Many European visitors might expect poverty, disorder or uniformity, yet Lagos reveals a far more complex reality. It is a megacity where economic power, creativity and innovation exist directly alongside inequality, overcrowding and infrastructural pressure.

The informal sector, for example, is often seen as chaotic or unregulated. This exhibition shows that it is actually a highly organised system that keeps the city functioning and provides livelihoods for millions. Instead of representing weakness, it demonstrates resilience, adaptability and entrepreneurial energy. Similarly, the creative industries — especially Nollywood — challenge the stereotype that African countries lack cultural influence. Lagos exports films, music and fashion across the continent and beyond, shaping global culture in ways that many outsiders underestimate.

At the same time, the exhibition makes visible the tensions that shape everyday life. Rapid population growth puts enormous pressure on transport, housing and public services. Communities like Makoko highlight the inequalities that emerge when urban development outpaces infrastructure. These contrasts show that Lagos is full of opportunities, but also full of challenges that cannot be ignored.

Overall, the exhibition demonstrates why Nigeria cannot be understood as one simple or homogeneous country. Lagos alone contains extreme diversity — in identity, living conditions, economic roles and cultural expression. For European visitors, this means looking beyond stereotypes and recognising the complexity, contradictions and human stories that define modern Nigeria.
